ABOUT SANICARE ORG

Every body deserves to be cared for, understood, and never made to feel ashamed.

We're a non-profit built by people who grew up without the products, the information, or the permission to talk about periods openly. Here's where we came from, and where we're going.

Who we are

SaniCare Org exists because access to something as basic as a pad or tampon still isn't guaranteed for every girl and woman. We hand out care packages, run hygiene education sessions, and go directly into communities that are too often left out of the conversation — because dignity shouldn't depend on your postcode or your income.

1980s

Where it started

Commercial sanitary products entered the market, changing what was possible for menstrual care. Before that, women and girls relied on reusable cloths and rags — resourceful, but rarely something anyone chose freely.

Early 90s

SaniCare Org is founded

We were established to close the gap between what had become available and who could actually afford it. From day one, our model has been simple: go to where the need is, not the other way around.

Since

Area by area, story by story

We travel from community to community delivering care packages and short, practical lessons on hygiene, while building a visual record of the people and places we've reached — so the impact doesn't go unseen.

Our mission

To create a safe, open environment for people who were never given the tools or the information to understand and care for their own bodies. We believe that not having the privilege of that knowledge shouldn't mean going without.

Our vision

A world where every woman and girl, wherever she lives, has what she needs to understand her body and care for it properly — and where hygiene products are treated as essential, not optional.

Who we serve

Our work is focused on people assigned female at birth — particularly those in under-resourced communities who face real financial and educational barriers to menstrual care. Care packages go beyond period products too, covering other everyday hygiene essentials our recipients need.

Why it matters

Hygiene products are still not free, even though every menstruating person faces the same monthly reality. Until that changes, we're stepping in — while also pushing for the policy change that would make this work unnecessary.

Who's behind it

Our team is made up of educated volunteers and staff who understand the communities we serve, because many of them come from those same communities. Lived experience shapes everything we do here.
